What the role asks for

The title is Director, but the job description describes building a rewards function from the ground up. Most Director roles run programs that already exist. This one is asked to create them:

  • Executive compensation design for a private company: base philosophy, annual incentive, long-term and retention incentives, payout rules and scenario modeling for leadership discussions
  • Job architecture and salary ranges across the enterprise: levels, midpoints, range widths, geographic differences and promotion guidelines
  • Dashboards and analytics that show range use, market position, incentive spend, pay equity, retention risk and offer competitiveness
  • Incentive governance: design, approval processes, calendars, calculations and communication
  • Decision rules leaders can apply consistently, built with the ELT, Finance, Legal, HR and Talent Acquisition

The job description also calls for "a risk-taker who has developed processes where none existed." That scope is why the recommendation sits in the upper half of the Director range rather than at the median.

The figures are rounded from 2026 US survey data. That survey leans toward technology companies, which tend to pay more than general industry. It was chosen on purpose: the analytics, dashboard and modeling work in this job description is the skill set those companies pay for. A general-industry comparison would put the Director median lower. Running both cuts side by side is a standard step before a final offer.

The structure is deliberate. The base sits modestly above the Director median, and the bonus is set above the usual range. More of the pay is at risk, so the top of the package is earned only when Mastronardi performs.

Built for a private company

The recommendation uses no equity. Mastronardi is family owned, and the job description asks for incentives that reward "enterprise performance, individual contribution, retention, and long-term value creation" in a private company.

In place of equity, it uses a cash long-term incentive of about $25,000 a year, paid on a multi-year cycle and tied to company results. That is about 11% of base, below the market norm at this level. The form, whether deferred cash, a retention award or performance units, would follow the company's own incentive framework.
