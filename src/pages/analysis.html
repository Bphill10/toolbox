Base is set at the role's base cap of $230,000. With a 35% target bonus, total target cash is $310,500. Mastronardi does not offer equity, so long-term incentive is $0 and total direct compensation is also $310,500, a market ratio of 1.03 to 1.13. The role maps to survey level M5 (Director).

Why above the median. This role builds a rewards function from scratch: executive compensation design, job architecture, enterprise salary ranges, incentive governance, and the dashboards and analytics behind them. That scope prices in the upper half of the Director market. Watch the bonus. At 35% the target is above the market's top quarter (25%). At the market's own 22%, the same base gives total target cash of $280,600, still above the median.

What could change the number

  • Bonus level. The market median target is 22% and the 75th percentile 25% to 26%. At 22%, a $230,000 base gives total target cash of $280,600, still above the median.
  • Scope. Building the function from scratch is what places the base above the median rather than at it.
  • No equity. Long-term incentive is $0, so total direct compensation equals total target cash. Against market medians that include equity, the package is at 1.03 to 1.13.
  • Survey choice. Radford leans toward technology companies, which pay more than general industry, but it fits this role's analytics skill set.
  • Blending surveys. Good practice blends only like-for-like matches. A hybrid role like this has no single clean match, the weak spot in benchmarking it. Blending in broader cuts would lower the number: combined compensation and benefits families pay less ($213,200 vs $221,450 median base) because they include benefits roles, diluting the premium for the scarcer skill set. Compensation is the anchor; the rest are cross-checks.
  • Location. These figures are US. A Canada-based role would need a Canadian cut.
  • Timing. Survey data is not aged; for an offer more than six months out, add about 3% a year (an assumption).
