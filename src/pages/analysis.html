Base is set at the role's base cap of $230,000. With a 35% target bonus, total target cash is $310,500, in the upper half of the market. Adding an annualized cash long-term incentive of about $25,000 (11% of base) brings total direct compensation to $335,500. The role maps to survey level M5 (Director).

Why above the median. This role builds a rewards function from scratch: executive compensation design, job architecture, enterprise salary ranges, incentive governance, and the dashboards and analytics behind them. That scope prices in the upper half of the Director market. Watch the bonus. At 35% the target is above the market's top quarter (25%). At the market's own 22%, the same base gives total target cash of $280,600, still above the median.

What could change the number

  • Bonus level. The market median target is 22% and the 75th percentile 25% to 26%. At 22%, a $230,000 base gives total target cash of $280,600, still above the median.
  • Scope. Building the function from scratch, including executive pay design, job architecture, ranges and analytics, is what places the base above the median rather than at it.
  • Private company. No equity is assumed. Long-term incentive is cash, tied to enterprise results.
  • Survey choice. Radford leans toward technology companies, which pay more than general industry. It fits the analytics and modeling skill set this role asks for; a general-industry cut would sit lower.
  • Location. These figures are US. A Canada-based role would need a Canadian cut.
  • Timing. Survey data is not aged. For an offer more than about six months after the survey date, age by roughly 3% a year (an assumption, not data).
