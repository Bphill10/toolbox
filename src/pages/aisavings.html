Step 4 · Analytics

AI time savings for every employee

Hours AI gives back to each job, what those hours are worth at that job’s pay, and how much of it becomes real savings by not refilling some roles as people leave. No layoffs in the model: the savings only come out of normal turnover and growth.

Who uses it, and how much time it saves them

Defaults start from the St. Louis Fed surveys: people who use generative AI save about 5.4% of their work hours, a little over two hours a week, and about four in ten workers now use it. Desk jobs sit above that, frontline hourly work well below. Change any number.

How the numbers are built

Time and its value
  1. Hours saved = people × 2,080 hours × share who use AI × share of their time it saves.
  2. Value of that time = hours saved × the job’s average pay ÷ 2,080. This is capacity, not cash: it shows up as faster work, more output, and less overtime.
  3. Full-time equivalents = hours saved ÷ 2,080.
Turning time into a smaller labor force
  1. Positions the time can cover = full-time equivalents × the share that replaces a hire.
  2. Capped by openings: never more than the people who leave in a year (today’s turnover) plus the new roles growth would add. That cap is what keeps it to attrition, with no layoffs.
  3. Savings = positions not filled × the job’s pay × (1 + benefits and taxes), less what the AI seats cost.
Proving it with Mastronardi’s own numbers
  • Pilot first. Give the tools to two or three teams for 90 days and time a handful of real tasks before and after: a job description, a monthly report, a schedule, a supplier email.
  • Replace the defaults. Put the measured adoption and time saved into the boxes above, by job, so the totals stand on company data instead of national surveys.
  • Track hires that weren’t needed. Each time a role opens, the manager records whether it was refilled, merged or covered by the team with AI. That log is the real savings number.
  • Watch the other side. Overtime, error rates and turnover in the teams carrying more work. Savings that come back as burnout aren’t savings.

Sources: St. Louis Fed, The Impact of Generative AI on Work Productivity (users save 5.4% of hours, 1.4% across all workers) and Generative AI, productivity and the future of work (adoption up from 28% to 39% of workers). Headcount, pay and turnover are fictional demo data for the last 12 months.
