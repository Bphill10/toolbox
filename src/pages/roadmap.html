The four steps

Step 1 · weeks 1 to 4

Get the market data and the job descriptions

Budget about $30,000 to $40,000 a year at participant pricesOutput priced benchmark jobs and a market read for the ELT

Nothing else can be built without it. Survey licenses belong to the company that buys them, so Mastronardi needs its own. Start with a small, well-matched set and add sources only where a gap shows up.

Starting points

  • Collect every job description we have, whatever the quality. A few sentences beat a job title alone. Run them through the JD Matcher to get first survey matches and levels.
  • Pick 30 to 50 benchmark jobs that cover most of the headcount: greenhouse operations, packing, distribution, plus the corporate and leadership roles.
  • Start lean, then participate. Mercer's 2026 window closed on April 24, and buying its 2026 data now costs about three times the participant price. So start with AgCareers, CompData and the free government data now, buy only the Mercer modules that fill a real gap, and participate in Mercer's spring 2027 cycle (about March to April) for full participant pricing.
  • For IT roles, use skill premiums on top of the general market rather than a separate tech survey.

Free cross-checks for hourly roles

  • US: BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics.
  • Canada: Job Bank wage tables. Ontario greenhouse labourers: $20.50 an hour median; greenhouse supervisors: $30.00.
  • Mexico: IMSS registered wages and INEGI's employment survey.

Step 1b · weeks 4 to 8

Competitive assessment

Budget $0, built in-houseOutput a one-page market position for the ELT, with the cost to fix the gaps

Once the benchmark data is in, compare what Mastronardi pays today with the market. This is the first real answer for the ELT: where pay is competitive, where it is not, and what closing the gaps would cost.

What it shows

  • Market ratio for base and total cash by job family, level, country and site.
  • Who sits below the 25th or above the 75th percentile, and how many people that is.
  • Hourly rates against local postings and minimum wages, site by site.
  • Pay compression, for example supervisors paid close to their crews.
  • Turnover laid over pay, to show where low pay is costing people.
  • How competitive should we be, by job family or by job: lag, match or lead the market, set from what the company values most and weighed against turnover and what it costs.
  • How much pay moves turnover here, measured from Mastronardi’s own data: turnover against market ratio, job by job, then checked against past raises. The tool starts at 15% fewer leavers for each 5% above market and swaps in the measured number.

Deliverables

  • A one-page summary for the ELT, with a few charts.
  • Cost-to-fix scenarios: bring everyone to range minimum, to the 25th percentile, or to target position.
  • A short list of urgent fixes for critical and hard-to-fill roles.
  • Built from the survey data with the How Competitive Should We Be, Pay Analysis and KPI Dashboards tiles in this demo.
Step 2 · months 1 to 4

Build the global job architecture

Budget $0 to $10,000 built in-house with AI; consultants only if neededOutput job families, levels, leveling guide, salary ranges by country

The foundation for consistent pay. Every job gets a family, a level and a range, so offers, promotions and merit follow one set of rules across the US, Canada and Mexico.

Starting points

  • Follow the survey's own grading and career levels rather than inventing a method. That keeps every job matched the same way the market data is cut.
  • Start basic: support, professional, management and executive, with a short plain-language guide for each level. Add detail only where it is needed.
  • Level the top layers (ELT and directors) and the largest populations first, then fill in the rest.
  • Match each job to survey codes from step 1, then build ranges: midpoints from the market, wider ranges at senior levels, and country differentials.
  • Load the structure into the HRIS so ranges show up in everyday decisions.
  • Publish an org chart and build succession plans for critical roles: who is ready now, in one to two years or later, and the development each needs.
  • Read the structure from the org chart: layers from the CEO to the front line, spans of control, one-over-one managers, and anyone graded at or above their own manager. Narrow teams get merged as managers leave, not by cutting.

Tool options

  • Build in-house with AI tools, like the Job Architecture Builder, JD Creator and Job Titles and Pay Ranges tiles in this demo.
  • The survey's career levels (Mercer's framework comes with the Benchmark Database), at no extra cost.
  • Consultant-led methods such as Mercer IPE or WTW Global Grading, only if the basic approach runs out.
  • HRIS: whichever system Mastronardi runs, to hold levels and ranges.
Step 3 · months 3 to 9

Design incentives: STI first, then LTI

Budget $0 to $10,000 in-house; $40,000 to $150,000 with an executive pay consultant; $15,000 to $50,000 legal for LTI plan documentsOutput STI plan and governance, LTI framework for leaders and critical talent

With levels in place, targets can be set by level instead of by negotiation. The short-term plan comes first because it affects the most people and runs every year.

Starting points

  • STI: eligibility and target bonus by level, aligned to the market; enterprise and individual measures; a payout curve with a threshold, target and 150% maximum; an approval calendar.
  • LTI for a private company: cash long-term plans, phantom equity or appreciation rights, and deferred retention awards for critical talent.
  • Model the cost at threshold, target and maximum before anything goes to the ELT.

Tool options

  • Modeling: the STI Plan and LTI Analyzer tiles in this demo, or Excel.
  • Executive pay consultants, optional: Pearl Meyer, Meridian, FW Cook, or the survey vendors' consulting arms.
  • Legal counsel for LTI plan documents and tax treatment in each country.
Step 4 · months 6 to 12, with quick reads from month 2

Analytics and dashboards

Budget covered by the AI tools below; no packaged softwareOutput leader dashboards and an annual pay cycle on one dataset

Once the data and structure exist, analytics shows whether it is working: who is paid where in their range, what incentives cost, where pay equity or retention risk sits.

Starting points

  • Quick reads in months 2 and 3: market position by job family, and the cost of fixing the biggest gaps.
  • Then the standing dashboards: range position and compa-ratio, market position, incentive spend, pay equity, retention risk, offer competitiveness.
  • An annual pay equity review and a merit cycle run from the same data.

Tool options

  • AI-built dashboards and tools, like this demo, built on the company's own data. No BI licenses and no packaged compensation software.
  • Merit matrix (in Merit Budget and Planning): increase by rating and position in range, fitted to budget.
  • Total Rewards Statements for every employee, in local currency, saved as PDF.
  • AI time savings for every job: hours given back, their value, and the roles that need not be refilled as people leave.

Security: is Azure required?

No. Azure isn’t what makes it safe; the controls below are. Azure is the easiest way to get all of them if Mastronardi already runs Microsoft 365, because company sign-in, storage and IT’s monitoring are already there. Any setup IT approves that meets the same list is just as safe.

  • Company sign-in: people log in with their Mastronardi Microsoft account (Entra ID), with multi-factor authentication and access managed by IT. No shared passwords.
  • Data stays in the tenant: HRIS extracts and survey files sit in Azure storage the company controls, with access logs.
  • AI inside the same boundary: Claude is available through Microsoft Foundry on Azure and is billed through the Microsoft agreement at standard API rates, so the AI tools sit under the same contracts and controls as the rest of IT.
  • Role-based access: leaders see their own organisation; pay detail and succession plans are limited to HR and the executive team.
  • AI under a business contract: on Claude’s business plans (Team, Enterprise and the API, including through Azure), Anthropic doesn’t train its models on company data by default and acts as the company’s data processor. API inputs are kept for 30 days, and zero retention can be arranged.
  • IT review first: security and IT sign off on the setup before any real employee data is loaded. This demo uses fictional data only.

What isn’t safe, with or without Azure: pay data pasted into personal AI accounts, spreadsheets emailed around, or real data on a demo host like this one behind a shared password.

Azure doesn't publish a fixed price for Static Web Apps Standard on its pricing page; the hosting figure is an estimate to confirm in the Azure pricing calculator.

The first 30 days

  1. Week 1: meet the ELT and Finance; confirm the fiscal year, the budget cycle and when the next bonus plan year starts.
  2. Week 1: pull an HRIS census: job, level, location, pay, bonus, hire date and manager for every employee.
  3. Weeks 1 and 2: collect every job description in the company, whatever the quality, and run them through the JD Matcher.
  4. Week 2: inventory what exists: pay practices, offer approvals, bonus and retention plans.
  5. Week 2: order the core surveys and sign up for the spring 2027 participation cycles.
  6. Weeks 3 and 4: match and price the 30 to 50 benchmark jobs.
  7. Weeks 4 to 8: the competitive assessment: where current pay sits against the market, and the cost to fix the gaps.
  8. End of month 2: the assessment goes to the ELT, with a confirmed plan for steps 2 to 4.
